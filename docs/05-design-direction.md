# 05 — Design Direction

## Brand
**Mian Tayyab Steel (MTS)**

## Design Goal
Create a premium industrial visual identity that makes Mian Tayyab Steel feel like a serious, established steel supplier and industrial business.

The design should feel:
- strong
- technical
- credible
- structured
- modern
- premium
- practical

It must not feel:
- playful
- experimental
- startup-like
- SaaS-like
- generic AI-generated
- overly futuristic

---

# 1. Core Visual Direction

The website should combine:

**Industrial authority + modern commercial clarity**

The reference site OHT may influence structure and content density, but MTS should have its own visual identity.

The design should communicate steel through:
- strong geometry
- clean lines
- structural layouts
- technical content
- real industrial imagery
- controlled contrast
- disciplined spacing

Avoid using literal steel textures everywhere.

Industrial identity should come primarily from composition, typography, imagery, and structure.

---

# 2. Colour Direction

## Primary Theme
**Blue-led industrial theme**

Do not use bright startup blue as the dominant visual language.

Preferred direction:

- deep navy / steel blue for authority
- medium industrial blue for interactive elements
- pale blue-grey for subtle background variation
- white / off-white for clean content areas
- charcoal / dark grey for technical contrast
- neutral steel greys for borders, tables, and supporting UI

Final HEX values should be selected during the design-system phase after visual comparison.

Do not arbitrarily introduce:
- purple gradients
- neon cyan
- pink
- strong green
- generic multi-colour gradient backgrounds

unless there is a genuine brand requirement later.

---

# 3. Logo Direction

## Brand Mark
**MTS**

Preferred logo direction:
- monogram or strong wordmark
- geometric
- industrial
- clean
- readable
- suitable for header, favicon, documents, signage, and social use

Possible inspiration:
- structural beams
- steel profiles
- engineered letter construction
- angular or modular geometry

Do not make the logo:
- overly detailed
- clipart-like
- metallic 3D
- aggressively bevelled
- dependent on gradients
- difficult to use at small sizes

The MTS mark should work in:
- blue on white
- white on blue
- monochrome
- favicon form

---

# 4. Typography Direction

Typography should feel professional and industrial, not decorative.

Preferred characteristics:
- strong headings
- excellent readability
- modern sans-serif family
- compact but not cramped
- confident weight hierarchy

Avoid:
- ultra-thin heading fonts
- display fonts that reduce readability
- overly futuristic tech fonts
- exaggerated condensed typography across the whole site

Heading scale should be controlled.

Do not use giant 70–100px headings simply to create a “premium” look.

Content density is important for this business.

---

# 5. Layout Philosophy

The website should use:
- disciplined grid systems
- clear content hierarchy
- deliberate asymmetry where useful
- practical whitespace
- strong image ratios
- technical tables where appropriate
- modular but non-repetitive sections

Do not make every section:
- centered
- full of cards
- rounded
- icon-led
- equal-height
- visually identical

Pages should be composed based on their content.

---

# 6. Homepage Direction

The homepage should immediately communicate:
- MTS brand
- steel / industrial business
- product depth
- credibility
- business capability
- clear inquiry path

The hero should not resemble a SaaS landing page.

Preferred hero approaches:
- strong industrial photography
- product-focused imagery
- structured copy
- practical CTAs
- restrained overlays
- controlled visual movement if needed

Possible hero content:
- company positioning
- major product categories
- primary inquiry CTA
- WhatsApp / quote CTA
- supporting credibility statement

Avoid:
- dashboard mockups
- fake floating product cards
- glow effects
- abstract 3D shapes
- decorative gradients with no industrial relevance

---

# 7. Product Presentation

Products are a central part of the site.

Product cards should prioritize:
- product image
- product name
- category
- short technical cue
- clear navigation to detail page

Product detail pages should support:
- gallery
- description
- specifications
- grades
- dimensions
- applications
- features
- related products
- FAQs
- quote / WhatsApp CTA

Technical information should feel easy to scan.

Tables and specification grids should use strong hierarchy and restrained styling.

---

# 8. Collection / Application Pages

Collection pages are not basic category archives.

They should feel like proper SEO and commercial landing pages.

They may contain:
- introduction
- relevant products
- application information
- industry use cases
- technical comparisons
- selection guidance
- FAQs
- brochure download
- quote CTA

Each collection page should have a distinct content rhythm while staying within the same design system.

---

# 9. Service Pages

Services should communicate capability and reliability.

Service pages may include:
- industrial imagery
- service description
- equipment/capability information
- use cases
- process
- benefits
- service FAQs
- contact CTA

Avoid generic icon grids unless the content genuinely benefits from them.

---

# 10. Imagery

Use real industrial and steel-related imagery wherever possible.

Preferred:
- steel coils
- beams
- sheets
- fabrication
- warehouses
- loading
- cutting
- machinery
- industrial applications
- construction
- manufacturing

Avoid:
- generic stock office teams
- fake handshake photos
- abstract technology imagery
- irrelevant city skylines
- generic AI illustrations

Images should support content, not decorate empty sections.

---

# 11. Motion

Motion should be restrained.

Acceptable:
- subtle section reveal
- image hover
- controlled slider transitions
- accordion animation
- menu transitions
- gallery transitions

Avoid:
- constant floating elements
- excessive parallax
- scroll-jacking
- text flying from multiple directions
- animated gradients
- animation on every section

---

# 12. Responsive Behaviour

Mobile should be designed intentionally.

Do not simply shrink desktop.

Special attention required for:
- product cards
- technical tables
- galleries
- navigation
- long-form collection content
- FAQs
- forms
- CTAs
- related products

Technical information may need stacked/mobile-specific treatment.

---

# 13. Visual Quality Test

Before approving any page, ask:

1. Does this look like a steel/industrial business?
2. Does it feel commercially credible?
3. Could this section belong to a random SaaS company?
4. Are the colours disciplined?
5. Is the typography controlled?
6. Does the content drive the layout?
7. Are we using real information instead of filler?
8. Is the design premium without becoming decorative?
9. Would a contractor or industrial buyer trust this company after viewing the page?

If the page looks like a generic AI template, redesign it.
